Step 1 of 5

Bosgo

The Threshold

The wooden beam at the base of the door frame is the bosgo — the threshold. Step over it, never on it, and lead with your right foot.

Why it matters: the threshold is believed to hold the household's stability. Stepping on it is considered a bad omen for the family inside.

Step 2 of 5

Nar Zöv

Moving Clockwise

Once inside, move to your left and travel clockwise around the interior. Never cut through the space between the two central support pillars, the bagana — that path is reserved.

Why it matters: the line between the pillars is a sacred axis connecting hearth to sky. Crossing it is seen as cutting through the family's fortune.

Step 3 of 5

Seating Order

Guests, Family, Elders

The west side (left of the door) is for guests and men. The east side (right) is for family and women. The back, or khoimor, is reserved for elders and sacred objects — wait to be shown your seat.

Why it matters: seating maps the family's own order. Sitting unguided, especially in the khoimor, is read as claiming a status that isn't yours.

4

Step 4 of 5

Golomt

The Hearth

Sit with your feet tucked back and soles pointed away from the stove and from any elder. Never pass anything over the hearth, and never put waste into it.

Why it matters: the golomt is the family's fire spirit and its literal source of warmth and food. Disrespecting it is considered a serious offense.

5

Step 5 of 5

Receiving

Tea, Snuff & Food

Accept whatever is offered with your right hand, elbow supported by your left. If you can't finish it, take a small symbolic sip or bite — an outright refusal is considered impolite.

Why it matters: offering food to a guest is a point of pride for the host family. Two hands, and a gesture of acceptance, return that respect.

Protocol complete

You're ready to step inside. A quick reminder of what you learned:

  • 1 Step over the bosgo, right foot first — never on it.
  • 2 Move clockwise (Nar Zöv); never cross between the two bagana pillars.
  • 3 Sit only where shown — west for guests, east for family, khoimor for elders.
  • 4 Keep your feet tucked away from the golomt hearth.
  • 5 Receive tea or food right-handed, left hand supporting your elbow.
